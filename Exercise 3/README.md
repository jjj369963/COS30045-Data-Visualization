# Exercise 3: Communicating Data Insights

## Data Story

For this project, my target audience is everyday shoppers who are looking to buy a new TV but want to keep an eye on their electricity bills. 

When people go TV shopping, they usually just look at the price tag and how big the screen is. They don't always think about the hidden long-term costs. I wanted to create a visualisation that answers two main questions for them:
1. How much more power do you actually use when you upgrade to a massive screen?
2. Are premium screens like OLED actually worse for your electricity bill compared to standard LCDs?

By showing this information clearly, I want to help consumers balance getting a nice TV with being energy efficient.

---

## About the Data

### Data Source
The data comes from the Australian Government's Energy Rating program (provided as part of the course materials). It lists different TV models available in Australia, including their screen sizes, display technology, and estimated annual energy consumption (kWh/year).

### Data Processing
To make the chart easy to understand, I grouped the TVs into three size categories: Small (under 50"), Medium (50" to 64"), and Large (65" and above). I also focused on the three most common screen types (Standard LCD, LED-backlit LCD, and OLED). I calculated the average energy use for each group so the audience can see the general trends without getting overwhelmed by hundreds of individual TV models.

### Privacy
There are no privacy issues with this project. The dataset only contains public product specifications for electronics. There is no personal or sensitive data about individual people's viewing habits.

### Accuracy and Limitations
The "annual energy consumption" in the data is just an estimate based on standard testing (like assuming the TV is on for a few hours a day). In real life, if someone leaves their TV on all day or turns the brightness all the way up, it will use a lot more power. Also, the dataset is a snapshot and might not include every single brand-new TV model in stores right now, but it's definitely enough to show the overarching trends.

### Ethics
I tried to make the visualisation as fair as possible. Instead of selectively choosing specific high or low-performing TV models to make a brand look bad, I used averages across the whole category. The goal is just to inform consumers objectively, not to trick them or push them toward a specific brand.
