Appliance Energy Consumption in Australia

Information and tools to help households understand their energy usage.

Overview

Household appliances make up a large portion of residential electricity use in Australia. According to the Australian Government's Energy Rating program, the average household consumes around 18–20 kWh per day, with heating, cooling, and refrigeration being the largest contributors.

Understanding how much energy each appliance uses can help households make better purchasing decisions and reduce their electricity bills over time.

One thing that often gets overlooked is standby power — devices that are plugged in but not being used can still draw electricity. This can account for up to 10% of a household's total electricity bill.

Electricity prices in Australia vary by state and provider. As of 2024, most households pay between 25 and 40 cents per kWh. The Australian Energy Rating program also provides a 1–10 star system for appliances, where more stars means more energy efficient.

For more information, the Australian Government's Energy Rating website lets you compare appliances and estimate running costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

A kilowatt-hour is the standard unit used on electricity bills. It equals using 1,000 watts of power for one hour. For example, a 100 W light bulb left on for 10 hours uses 1 kWh.
Heating and cooling systems are typically the largest consumers, followed by water heaters, refrigerators, washing machines, and dishwashers. Televisions also contribute, especially larger models left on for long periods.
Prices vary by state and provider. As of 2024, most households pay between 25 and 40 cents per kWh. Checking your electricity bill will give you the most accurate figure for your situation.
Switch appliances off at the wall instead of leaving them on standby, choose higher star-rated appliances when buying, and run washing machines and dishwashers during off-peak hours.
The Energy Rating label (1–10 stars) shows how efficiently an appliance uses energy compared to similar models. More stars means less energy used for the same task, which can make a significant difference in running costs over time.
